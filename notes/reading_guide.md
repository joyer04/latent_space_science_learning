# Reading Guide

This guide is meant to support a flexible reading process rather than a strict sequence. The document can be read linearly, but it also works well as a notebook to move through by theme.

## Suggested Reading Order

1. Begin with the introduction or framing sections to understand the overall motivation.
2. Continue with sections that discuss the shape or organization of representation spaces.
3. Pay attention to examples that connect geometry with semantic structure.
4. Return later to denser or more speculative sections after the main ideas have settled.

## Key Themes

### Geometry

Look for places where the text suggests that internal representations may have structure that can be studied spatially, relationally, or topologically.

### Semantics

Notice where meaning seems to emerge in directions, clusters, or transformations inside a latent space.

### Structure

Track any recurring idea that representations may not be arbitrary, but organized in ways that can be described, compared, or navigated.

## A Non-Linear Approach Is Fine

It is reasonable to read this document out of order. One useful approach is:

- skim for sections that immediately raise questions
- mark passages that suggest testable intuitions
- move between the PDF, your own notes, and the open questions in this repository

The aim is not mastery in one pass, but gradual familiarity with the conceptual landscape.
